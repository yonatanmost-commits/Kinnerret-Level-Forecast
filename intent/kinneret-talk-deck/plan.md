# Plan: Kinneret Conference talk deck
From: intent/kinneret-talk-deck/spec.md v5 (v4 accepted in chat 2026-10-13; R27/A19 frontier slide added the same day by the originator's choice).

## Design
- D1. Deck build: a single markdown source, `talk.md`, holds every slide (its one object, its on-slide text, its speaker notes). A python-pptx builder turns it into the PPTX. Chosen over a Claude Slides artifact (notes, audio and timings are hard to control) and over hand-building in PowerPoint (every edit would be manual), because the slide-by-slide walk-through (R23) then costs one source edit and a rebuild per change.
- D2. PowerPoint itself (scripted from Python) exports the PDF, renders single-slide pictures for the walk-through, and produces the narrated rehearsal copy (each slide's audio embedded, timings set). Chosen over a third-party converter because the real renderer is the proof for fonts and layout.
- D3. The freeze measurements live in a separate script, `Automation/talk_freeze_run.py`, which imports the existing `run_cv*` functions from `08_train_forecast_model.py`, cuts the gold table at the freeze date, times each model, and writes one numbers ledger. Chosen over adding flags to the training script, so the daily production pipeline stays untouched. Follows the `Automation/redline_backtest.py` → `docs/*.json` pattern.
- D4. The GRU is revived inside the talk script by porting `run_cv_gru` from git (`4f9e553^`, the last commit before its retirement), using `Automation/gru_model.py` (still on disk, torch 2.12 CPU installed). Ruled by the originator 2026-10-13: all 8 models come from one snapshot with real runtimes.
- D5. Two baselines, both scored on the champion's walk-forward folds and rows. On stage: zero change ("the lake stays the same"). In the ledger and the Q&A sheet: the seasonal average (each day-of-year's mean volume change, computed from the training years of each fold only). Ruled by the originator 2026-10-13. Chosen over zero change alone, which a technical listener may call a strawman.
- D6. Numbers flow only through the ledger. `talk.md` and the Q&A sheet use placeholders like `{champion_r2}`, and the builder fills them in. The build stops on an unknown placeholder, and on any digit in the text that is not a placeholder (A7). Plain non-facts (the conference date, "15 minutes", years used as names such as "2023") go in a short allow-list at the top of `talk.md`, so every exception is visible in one place.
- D7. The ledger is the one persisted format the other parts depend on: a flat JSON of `key → {value, display, unit, source, date, kind}`, where `kind` is `freeze` (date = the freeze date) or `historical` (date = when it was originally measured, shown on the slide). Everything about how a number was measured stays inside the freeze script.
- D8. "Only when the facts change" is enforced by a fingerprint, not by memory. The freeze script runs the truth tests at the end of every run and, when they pass, writes `docs/talk/truth_stamp.json` holding a hash of the ledger. The builder compares that hash with the current ledger: if they match, the facts are already proven and nothing re-runs; if not, the build stops and says the truth tests must pass first. Chosen over running the tests on every build (slow, and pointless when numbers don't move — originator 2026-10-13) and over a manual "remember to re-test" step (forgettable).
- D9. Charts are drawn with matplotlib from the ledger only, never from the raw data, so every plotted value is a ledger value. Their look follows the `dataviz` skill, adapted to the light theme: muted series, and one accent colour reserved for the red line and the champion.
- D10. The visual base is light, airy and minimal: a white or near-white background, dark type, generous margins, one object centred per slide, and full-bleed metaphor pictures. Fonts are 28 pt and up for slide text and 20 pt and up for chart labels.
- D11. Metaphor pictures are AI-generated. **Superseded 2026-10-13: the free Gemini key has no image quota, so the originator generates each picture by hand.** The builder writes one prompt per picture in `docs/talk/images/<name>.prompt.txt`, in the style of the originator's `images/centaur.png`: a light watercolour-and-ink sketch on cream paper, the Kinneret and olive trees in the background, and empty space on one side. The originator saves the result as `<name>.png`. The notes still say the picture is AI-made. Original text: with Gemini, using `GEMINI_API_KEY` in the gitignored `.env` (in place 2026-10-13). Ruled by the originator 2026-10-13. Each prompt is stored beside its picture, and the speaker notes say the picture is AI-made. If the key turns out to have no image model, fall back to free stock photos with credits and tell the originator.
- D12. The walk-through runs in chat. The originator says what to change; Claude edits `talk.md`, the chart or the picture prompt, rebuilds, and opens that one slide as a picture (`build_deck.py --slide N`). Ruled by the originator 2026-10-13. Acceptance is recorded in `talk.md` as a slide's `accepted:` line holding a short hash of the slide's content, so the builder lists any slide changed since it was accepted (A12) and nothing is accepted silently.
- D13. Claude's two review passes — the style checklist (A8) and the claims-against-evidence read (A18) — are written after each full build to `docs/talk/review_notes.md`, one line per finding, each marked open, fixed or overruled by the originator. Review notes, not tests: they never block a build.
- D14. The build warnings (A9, A10) are printed at the end of every build: PPTX/PDF slide count and order, missing fonts or blank charts in the PDF, and for a rehearsal build the total timing and per-slide audio match. Warnings, never failures.
- D15. The narrated rehearsal uses the /TTL skill's `tts.py` on each slide's notes separately, so each slide's audio length sets its timing. Audio is cached per slide by content hash, so a rebuild only re-narrates changed slides. The rehearsal PPTX and its audio stay out of git and out of the submission.
- D17. The frontier slide (R27) reuses stored results, not new runs: the freeze script copies the 14–30-day wet/dry skill numbers from `docs/longrange_phaseb_results.json` into the ledger as `historical`, dated by the git commit that last changed that file, with a `measured_note` field ("measured 2026-06-22 (paper §4.3); first committed 2026-08-15"), which `numbers_check.md` shows beside the date because the file's first commit is a later deploy commit, and with a `perfect_prognosis: true` flag that the chart turns into a visible "best case — if we knew the weather ahead" label. The 7-day point is the freeze champion. The summer point is the crossing hindcast (originator's choice, 2026-10-13), run by the freeze script on the freeze as `freeze` entries: importing `load_daily`, `recession_by_doy`, `OBS_WINDOW` and `SCALE_CLAMP` from `Automation/redline_backtest.py` unchanged; crossings = days the daily level first drops below −213.0 m, from 2006 to the freeze; last rain = the last day before the crossing with ERA5 `rainfall_mm` ≥ 5 mm (`Silver Data/Meteorological/era5_kinneret_daily.csv`); every day from last rain to the day before the crossing, project with the leave-one-year-out recession (scaled by the trailing 21 days, and unscaled as the baseline) until the level drops below −213.0 (cap 400 days, else "never crossed"); the error is predicted minus actual days; the ledger stores the mean absolute error per lead-time band (0–7, 7–14, 14–30, 30–60, 60–90, 90–120, 120+ days) for both, the number of crossings, and the never-crossed count, also per band for each method; because the two methods miss different forecasts (91 vs 72 at the freeze), any spoken scaled-vs-unscaled comparison uses only the forecasts both methods brought to the line (paired MAE per band, stored as `crossing.paired.*`), and an MAE is spoken as an average, never as a bound (review of 1136afb, 2026-10-13). Planner's reference run (2026-10-13, scratch): scaled 0.8 / 1.0 / 1.8 / 6.8 / 21.5 / 21.7 / 21.2 days over 8 crossings. Chosen over re-running the long-range evaluation on the freeze (new heavy runs on a low-memory machine, and out of scope as modelling work) and over a words-only wall (the originator chose the slide, 2026-10-13).
- D18. The opening (R13, R28–R31, originator 2026-10-13):
  - **Title slide picture:** the app's own lake picture, `build_lake_svg(level)` from `kinneret_app/app_utils.py`, called with the freeze level and turned into a picture for the slide. The builder chooses how to rasterize it. No number on it.
  - **Hook chart:** redrawn as the app front page's year-over-year overlay (`kinneret_app/app.py`, "Year-over-Year Comparison"), restyled to the light deck look (D10). It shows each year from 2020 by day of year, the freeze year in the accent colour ending on the freeze date, and the red line. There is no level label. `chart_data("hook")` keeps its locked contract (dated series ending on the freeze date, red line, freeze level), so A6 holds unchanged.
  - **Winter scenarios:** computed by the freeze script as `historical` ledger entries `scenario.{dry,wet}.{season,change_m,spring_m}`, dated by the winter's end.
    - Window: the last 20 winters, 2006–07 to 2025–26, matching the crossing hindcast.
    - A winter's change is the level on 31 May of the next year minus the level on the freeze calendar date. It can be negative.
    - Dry is the smallest change, wet the largest. `spring_m` is the freeze level plus that change.
    - The first definition (the spring peak minus the start level, reviewing ee42df4 on 2026-10-13) was wrong for dry winters. A peak can't fall below the start, so 2024–25 scored a rise of 0.00 and "the lake stays where it is". In fact it fell 0.77 m by 31 May.
    - Chosen over percentiles (harder to say aloud) and over a model forecast (the talk's own message is that a season can't be forecast).
  - **The consequences** for desalination, water quality, algae and the wider circles are spoken qualitatively with no numbers (originator: "no facts to get wrong").
  - **The centaur slide** is a Gemini metaphor picture (D11). Kasparov's 2005 freestyle tournament and Tao's AI-assisted mathematics are told without quotes on the slide. The years go in the allow-list with a source in `numbers_check.md`, so Adnan can check them.
  - **QR code:** drawn with `segno` (pure Python, pip install), pointing to the app URL the originator gives. It sits on the who and closing slides.
  - **Today's level:** spoken from the app on the day. The notes hold the marker "[today's level, from the app]", never a digit, so A7 is unaffected.
- D19. One weekly yardstick (R10–R12, originator 2026-10-13):
  - **The finding:** a review found that `run_cv` scores the old champion one day at a time with the real lag changes, while the other seven runners score days 1–7 from one start day. `gbr_max_chain` (0.67) is a near relative, not the same models: it uses 150 trees and no signed-log target. So `baseline_gbr` is scored weekly in its own right (ruling Q1): its own `run_cv` models, log target included, run through `_simulate_7d_chain` and cached as a new entry. That makes 8 weekly runners.
  - **The new champion:** the freeze script takes the champion as the highest weekly mean among the 8 runners (`gbr_s1_direct_s2_anchor` at the freeze). `champion.*` keys are written from that runner, with `champion.model` naming it.
  - **Baselines:** zero change and the seasonal average are re-scored on the champion's direct test rows, one row per start day and horizon. Zero change predicts 0. The seasonal average is the day-of-year mean of the target day, from training years only.
  - **The timeline is weekly (ruled 2026-10-13, superseding the one-day timeline).** `history.<sha>.r2` is read from the champion's own entry, `models[champion.model].cv_vol_r2_mean`, in each git snapshot of `docs/olympics_results.json`. A snapshot whose value equals the previous snapshot's is a carried copy: `--winner-only` refreshes only the winner, so the August commits carry June's 0.702 forward. Such a snapshot is skipped. The timeline ends on `champion.r2`. At the freeze: c3a72b3 0.680, ac2ede5 0.698 (the Wade Storm step), 56d0555 0.700, 2da9bd7 0.702, freeze 0.707. The one-day score moves to its own slide (R33). Keys are `day_ahead.model.{r2,mae,n_<fold>}` (from `baseline_gbr`'s `run_cv`, cached) and `day_ahead.{zero_change,seasonal}.{r2,r2_<fold>}`, scored on those same rows. The first freeze run's baselines were already on those rows (−0.024 and 0.313). There is also `day_ahead.margin.zero_change.r2`. Each carries `yardstick: one_day`. The one-day slide uses a new `chart_data("one_day")`. The truth test file is unlocked again so the builder can revise A5 and add A21 (it re-locks the file and sends READY tests-locked).
  - **Locked tests:** the truth test file hard-codes `baseline_gbr` as the champion (A4) and ends the timeline on `champion.r2` (A5). The originator unlocks it, the builder revises A3, A4, A5 and A19 to this definition and re-locks it, then the freeze re-runs from the model cache (only the baselines are new).
  - Chosen over keeping the one-day champion with its chained score beside it (it no longer wins, so the race loses its point) and over re-measuring the whole history weekly (re-running old commits, too heavy before 1 Nov).
- D16. The talk files live in `docs/talk/`, with the builder beside its content. Follows `docs/paper/md_to_docx.py` beside `Kinneret_Forecast_Methods_Results.md`.

## Files that change
- `tests/test_talk_truth.py` (new, locked): the truth tests A2–A6 and A19, run against the ledger, the charts' data and the level file.
- `tests/test_talk_gates.py` (new, locked): A1 (the build stops when the ledger's hash doesn't match the truth stamp, and does not re-run the truth tests when it does) and A7 (the build stops on a typed-in number), using small fixture decks.
- `Automation/talk_freeze_run.py` (new): the freeze re-run of all 8 models with runtimes, both baselines, the hook's level facts, and the R² history (7 git snapshots as `historical`, plus the freeze point), and the frontier's stored long-range and summer numbers as `historical`. Writes the ledger, runs the truth tests, writes the stamp.
- `docs/talk/numbers_ledger.json` (new, generated): every number the talk may use.
- `docs/talk/truth_stamp.json` (new, generated): the ledger hash the truth tests last passed on.
- `docs/talk/talk.md` (new): the source of truth for slides, on-slide text, the picture or chart per slide, speaker notes with placeholders, the allow-list, and each slide's `accepted:` line.
- `docs/talk/charts.py` (new): the hook level chart, the Olympics R²-vs-runtime chart, the annotated R² timeline, the 1-week-vs-zero-change chart, and the frontier chart, all from the ledger.
- `docs/talk/make_images.py` (new): generates the metaphor pictures from stored prompts.
- `docs/talk/images/` (new): the generated pictures and their prompts.
- `docs/talk/build_deck.py` (new): talk.md + ledger + charts + pictures → `talk.pptx` and `talk.pdf`; `--slide N` renders one slide picture; `--rehearsal` builds `talk_rehearsal.pptx`.
- `docs/talk/review_notes.md` (new): Claude's checklist and claims findings per build.
- `docs/talk/qa_prep.md` (new): the Q&A prep sheet, using placeholders.
- `docs/talk/numbers_check.md` (new, generated): every number used and where it appears, with its date — for the originator (A11) and Adnan (A14).
- `.gitignore` (changed): ignore `docs/talk/talk_rehearsal.pptx`, `docs/talk/audio/` and `docs/talk/slide_*.png`.

## Order of work

3. Draw the five charts from the ledger.
4. 5. Generate the metaphor pictures.
6. First full build: `talk.pptx` and `talk.pdf`, the build warnings, and Claude's review notes (A8, A18). **Target: by 20 Oct 2026 (A17).**
7. Walk-through, slide by slide, until every slide is accepted (A12). Facts only change through step 2's script, so a fact change during the walk-through re-runs the freeze, not the edit loop.
8. Build the narrated rehearsal; the timing warning shows the total, and talk.md is trimmed and re-walked until it is 14:00–16:00 (A10). **Checkpoint: the originator listens (A13), and the outside listener retells it.**

## Risks
- **Riskiest step: the story itself (step 4).** Fluency, interest and the hook can't be machine-tested. The guards: the script checkpoint before any visual work, the walk-through, the rehearsal listen, and the outside-listener retell.
- The schedule is tight: steps 1–6 in a week, to leave five walk-through days before 25 Oct. The guard: pictures wait for the script checkpoint, so none are made for slides that get cut. Everything runs one step at a time, never in parallel, because the machine is constantly low on memory (originator, 2026-10-13); the freeze run, with the ~15-min GRU, runs alone with nothing else heavy open.
- The GRU port may not run as it did in June, after code drift. The guard: port the CV function from `4f9e553^` as is; if it still fails, stop and ask rather than quietly using the June constant.
- A commit of `docs/olympics_results.json` dated after the freeze would add a timeline snapshot later than the freeze point and fail A5. Nothing commits that file today (it has stayed dirty since the 2026-10-12 retrain). The guard: the freeze date is flexible (R9), so if such a commit lands, the freeze moves past it and everything is re-run together.
- The frontier slide mixes freeze and historical numbers on one chart, which can read as like-for-like. The guard: each historical point carries its date on the chart, the horizons are measured differently (R² on volume change vs. skill vs. MAE in mm), so the chart shows skill-or-not per horizon, not one shared axis of raw scores, and the script says so.
- Zero-change persistence on volume change will likely score R² near or below zero. That's correct but needs one plain sentence on stage; the seasonal baseline in the Q&A sheet is the honest backup.
- The typed-number guard (D6) may catch harmless words like "one" or ordinal dates. The guard: it scans digits only, and the allow-list is visible and short; every addition to it is named in the review notes.
- Gemini image generation may not be available on this key. The guard: the stock-photo fallback in D11.
- The free-tier TTS quota could stall the rehearsal build. The guard: per-slide audio cached by content hash.
- Scripting PowerPoint can hang on a modal dialog. The guard: run it hidden, with a timeout, and always quit PowerPoint in a `finally` block.
- The 15-minute fit may force cutting arc segments. The guard: the trim comes from the rehearsal timing, and any cut segment is named to the originator, not dropped silently.
- R² on volume change can be misheard as level R². The guard: the script says "daily volume change" the first time, and the Q&A sheet covers it.
