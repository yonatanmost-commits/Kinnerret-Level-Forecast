# Spec: Kinneret Conference talk deck
From: intent/kinneret-talk-deck/intent.md v1.

## Behaviour
- R1. The talk tells one story along the spine's arc: a cold open on the lake with no introduction (R13, R28), the forecasting question, the centaur (R29), who we are (R30), the build-up (R32: why a week, the data sources, the pipeline, the model), the soft drop (Model Olympics), the pivot (Wade Storm, who came after the Olympics), the data story, the 1-week results, the long-term wall, and the close that returns to the hook's graph and to the centaur.
- R2. Spoken at a calm pace, the talk runs about 15 minutes, leaving at least 3 minutes of the 20-minute slot for Q&A.
- R3. The language is common and spoken. No unexplained jargon reaches the audience. Any technical term that stays (R², persistence, gradient boosting) is explained in plain words the first time it is used.
- R4. Every slide has exactly one object to look at: a picture, a graph or, occasionally, a table.
- R5. Some slides are a single picture with no text, carrying a metaphor the speaker talks about.
- R6. Slides are super light on text, set in large fonts readable from the back of a hall.
- R7. The deck holds a data-driven storytelling tension: "a data-driven, truthful tale, told by a master storyteller". Every claim is true to the data, and the telling is a story, not a report.
- R8. The deck says "neural network vs. classic ML", never "AI vs. ML", and not "deep learning", which is less familiar to this audience.
- R9. Every number shown or spoken is re-measured on one data freeze, with a single stated date used for all numbers, and traces to a stored result from that run, or it is a historical value shown with its original date. The date is flexible: 2026-10-11 is the default, and it may move when that is convenient (for example, to reuse a run that already exists), as long as everything moves together.
- R10. All 8 Model Olympics configurations are run on the same freeze and scored on one weekly yardstick: each forecast starts from one day's real state and predicts days 1–7 without seeing the lake's real changes inside the week, and all seven days are scored. Every model uses the weather that actually happened, so every score is a best case, and the talk says so. The champion is the best model on that yardstick, whichever it is (at 2026-10-13, the s1-direct/s2-anchor gradient boosting at about 0.71). The old one-day-at-a-time score is never compared with weekly scores. Each model's training runtime is measured, and the Olympics slide shows R² against runtime.
- R11. At the 1-week horizon, the champion is compared against a persistence baseline scored under the same walk-forward CV and the same weekly yardstick, on the same forecasts. The talk states the margin.
- R12. One slide shows the score over time as an annotated timeline. Each step is labelled with its cause (model change or data change). The timeline is the weekly champion's own history on the weekly yardstick, read from the stored results in git: each value at the date it was first measured, ending on the freeze champion. A later snapshot that only carried a value forward unchanged is not a new point (at 2026-10-13: 0.680 on 30 May, 0.698 on 31 May, 0.700 and 0.702 on 1 Jun, 0.707 at the freeze). The one-day-at-a-time scores (0.694 → 0.771) are not shown. (Originator 2026-10-13, superseding the one-day timeline.)
- R22. The talk tells the Dr. Wade Storm episode as evidence for the pivot, under the name "the newest oldest trick in the book": telling the AI "you are an X specialist…". An AI expert persona (Claude in the role of a hydrology and meteorology specialist) first warned that 2023 scored worst for every model and might be a data problem, not a model problem. Following that up found that a whole year of rain had silently become zero (the lake gauge was offline, and four small pipeline bugs turned its missing readings into 0 mm instead of about 505 mm). With the data fixed, the persona's six simple "Signal Harvest" fixes followed. Together the rain fix and the six fixes raised the weekly champion's R² from 0.680 to 0.698, measured on 2026-05-31 (the largest step in its history), and the talk credits the gain to both, not to the six fixes alone. The persona is presented honestly as AI role-play, and the fixes as validated by measurement, not by the persona's authority.
- R13. The talk opens straight on the lake. The title slide is the lake picture from the app's front page (the lake outline filled to its level, with the red line). The next slide is the app front page's year-over-year level chart with the red line, drawn through the freeze date, showing the lake is not in a good spot. No level number is printed on either slide, because the deck is sent days before the talk: the speaker checks the app that morning and says today's level aloud. Any "worst in N years" claim matches the data.
- R14. The close names the specific data that was missing or inaccessible.
- R15. The long-term wall is framed as "here's what's missing", not "here's where I failed". It carries 2–3 transferable challenges, each with a "this applies to you" line.
- R16. The title slide names Adnan Agbaria as co-author.
- R33. A one-day slide comes right before the one-week slide (originator 2026-10-13): how well tomorrow's change can be told, given today's real lake state and the real weather. It shows the day-ahead score of the two-stage gradient boosting (about 0.76 at the freeze) against zero change and the seasonal average, all scored on the same day-ahead rows. It is presented as a different question (tomorrow, not a week), never as a model competing in the weekly Olympics, and leads into the week: skill fades with distance, which sets up the frontier.
- R32. Before the Olympics the talk builds up slowly, one slide each:
  - **Why a week:** the model is fed a weather forecast, and weather forecasts are good for about a week.
  - **The data sources:** the three national authorities.
  - **The pipeline:** the treatment-train slide.
  - **The model:** in plain words, rain and river first, then the lake's daily change.
- R28. On the year-over-year slide the speaker gives two one-line scenarios for the coming winter, a dry one and a wet one, each taken from a real past winter in the record (not a forecast), and each with its consequence for national water management told qualitatively: reliance on desalination, water quality and algae, and the wider circles of influence. No numbers or specific facts beyond the stored results. The slide ends on the forecasting question: which winter is coming, and wouldn't it help decisions to know?
- R29. A centaur slide follows: a classic metaphor picture, no text. The speaker tells Kasparov's centaurs (a human and a computer playing as one team, and amateurs with a good process beating stronger players and machines in 2005) and Terence Tao working with AI on mathematics. It is never worded as "weak human". The close pays it off.
- R30. Then, short and modest, but never weak: what I'm not (a meteorologist, a data analyst, a software engineer), what I am (a process engineer), and Adnan Agbaria as co-author. This slide and the closing slide carry a QR code to the online app, and the speaker says the app shows today's numbers.
- R31. The freeze date is named aloud where the data part begins: every number from there on is frozen on that date. The one exception is today's lake level, spoken from the app on the day and never written on a slide or in the notes as a number.
- R17. The deck exists as PPTX and as PDF, with the same content in both.
- R18. Every slide carries speaker notes, and together the notes form the full spoken script.
- R19. A Q&A prep sheet lists the likely hard questions, each with a prepared answer that matches the freeze numbers.
- R20. A narrated rehearsal PPTX plays the script as TTS audio (via /TTL) on auto-timed slides. Each slide's timing matches its narration, and it is kept private.
- R21. Adnan receives the draft by 25 Oct 2026 at the latest. After his review, the slides go to the organisers by 1 Nov 2026.

- R23. After the first full build, the user and Claude walk through the deck slide by slide. For each slide the user comments, Claude revises it and rebuilds, and the user sees the rebuilt slide as a picture of the real slide. A slide is done when the user says so. The first build is the middle of the lane, not its end.
- R24. Four judges accept the deck: Claude, the user, Adnan and the conference team. Each judges two things, each in their own way: is it true? (every number is real and honest, and no claim outruns its evidence) and is it interesting? (the story is told well). The lane ends when all four have accepted.
- R25. Numbers reach the slides, notes and Q&A sheet only from the stored results of the one freeze. A number typed straight into the text is refused when the deck is built.
- R26. The first full build is ready by 20 Oct 2026, leaving at least five days of slide-by-slide walk-through before the draft goes to Adnan on 25 Oct.
- R27. The long-term wall carries one frontier slide: a single chart of how far ahead the lake can be forecast — the 7-day champion (skilful), 14–30 days (wet-season skill only, from catchment-state memory, the soil bucket; temperature alone has none year-round; measured with the temperatures that actually happened, so it is a best case, an upper bound, not a working forecast, and the slide and script say so plainly, e.g. "if we knew the weather ahead"), and the summer red line, told as a retrospective crossing hindcast: for every crossing of −213.0 m since 2006, the summer method (an anomaly-scaled seasonal climatology, not machine learning) is re-issued every day from the last rain to the day before the crossing, and the chart shows how far off the predicted crossing date was by days of warning (at 2026-10-13 roughly: within 2 days inside a month, about 7 days at 30–60, about 3 weeks beyond 60, where it loses to the plain seasonal average). It ties the abstract's "rain-gated predictability frontier" to the talk's message: the frontier is where the data runs out. Its 14–30-day numbers are historical, shown with their original dates; the crossing hindcast is re-measured on the freeze.

## Acceptance criteria
### Claude: is it true? (locked tests, run only when the facts change)
- A1 (R24). Given a change to the facts (a new freeze date, a re-run of the freeze measurements, or a change to the stored numbers), when the truth tests run, then A2–A6 all pass. A change to wording, pictures or layout alone does not re-run them.
- A2 (R9, R19, R25). Given every number in the slides, notes and Q&A sheet, when each is looked up, then it maps to a stored result from the one stated freeze, recorded with its source and the freeze date, or to a historical value recorded with its source and original date and labelled with that date wherever it appears.
- A3 (R10). Given the freeze re-run, when it completes, then all 8 configurations have a weekly-yardstick R² (mean and per fold) and a measured runtime, the champion is the configuration with the highest mean, and the Olympics chart plots exactly those values.
- A4 (R11). Given the freeze data, when persistence and the seasonal average are scored on the champion's own weekly forecasts (same folds, same start days, same days 1–7), then its R² and MAE are recorded, and the slide or script states the champion-minus-persistence margin from them.
- A5 (R12, R22). Given the weekly champion's R² history (its first-measured values in the git snapshots plus the freeze champion), when the timeline is drawn, then every point sits at its date and value and every step carries a cause label. The 0.680 → 0.698 step is labelled as the Wade Storm step (the 2023 rain fix plus the Signal Harvest fixes), and both its numbers are re-checked against the freeze or labelled with their original date.
- A20 (R28, R9). Given the level record through the freeze, when the two winter scenarios are computed, then each is a real past winter in the record, stored in the ledger as historical with its season, its change in level from the freeze calendar date to the end of May and its source, the dry one is the smallest and the wet one the largest change in the stated window (a dry winter can lower the lake), and the notes speak only those values.
- A21 (R33, R9). Given the freeze, when the day-ahead scores are recorded, then the model's score and both baselines are scored on the same day-ahead rows per fold, stored as `day_ahead.*` freeze entries carrying their yardstick, and the one-day slide plots exactly those values.
- A6 (R13). Given the level record through the freeze date, when the hook is drawn, then it shows the red line at −213.0 m and the freeze-date level, and any "lowest in N years" claim is true for that calendar date. At 2026-10-11 the level is −213.655 m, which supports "lowest since 2018" and refutes "worst in a decade".
- A7 (R25). Given any build, when the text contains a number that does not come from the stored results, then the build stops and names it. This is an instant text scan, not a re-measurement, so it runs on every build.
- A18 (R7, R22, R24). Given the script, when Claude reads every claim against its evidence, then none outruns it: Wade Storm is presented as AI role-play, the fixes are credited to measurement, and the limits (the long-term wall, R² on volume change) are stated openly. Claude reports any overclaim as a review note. This is a read-through, not a locked test.
- A19 (R27, R9). Given the stored long-range and summer red-line results, when the frontier chart is drawn, then every plotted value equals a stored result recorded in the numbers ledger as historical, with its source and original date, the 14–30-day point is labelled on the chart as a best case (perfect-prognosis temperatures), the 7-day point is the freeze champion, and the crossing-hindcast values come from a run on the freeze, recorded with the number of crossings and the last-rain rule, and labelled as a seasonal method, not machine learning.

### Claude: is it interesting? (a review checklist, not locked tests)
- A8 (R1, R3–R8, R14–R16, R18, R19, R22). Given a build, when Claude reads it against the style checklist, then Claude reports which items hold and which don't, as review notes the user can overrule. The checklist: the arc runs in spine order and the last slide shows the hook's graph again; one picture, graph or table per slide; at most 12 words of slide text; fonts of 28 pt and up for slide text and 20 pt and up for chart labels; at least 3 picture-only slides, each with its metaphor in the notes; every technical term explained at first use or absent; no "AI vs. ML"; no "deep learning" outside the Q&A sheet, and the GRU labelled a "neural network"; the Wade Storm story says it is an AI persona, tells the 2023 warning and the missing year of rain, names one of the six fixes in plain words, and gives the combined gain with its date; the close names pumping/abstraction data and one other missing series; the long-term wall has 2–3 challenges, each with a "this applies to you" line and none worded as a failure; the title slide names Adnan Agbaria as co-author; the opening has no introduction before the year-over-year slide, no level number on any slide, the scenarios carry no numbers or facts outside the ledger, the centaur slide has no text and no "weak human" wording, the who slide says what I'm not before what I am, the QR code is on the who and closing slides, and the freeze date is named where the data begins; every slide has notes, and they read as one script; the Q&A sheet covers "why not an LSTM / deep learning?", "is R² on level or on volume?", "how do you handle pumping?" and "how far ahead can you actually see?".

### Build checks (reported as warnings, never blocking)
- A9 (R17). Given a build, when it finishes, then it reports whether the PPTX and PDF have the same slide count and order, and whether every chart and font rendered in the PDF.
- A10 (R2, R20). Given a rehearsal build, when it finishes, then it reports the total auto-timing (target 14:00–16:00) and whether each slide advances when its narration ends. The rehearsal file stays out of the submission, and the TTS key stays in the gitignored `.env`.

### The user: is it true? and is it interesting?
- A11 (R9, R24). Given a readable list of every number in the talk and where it appears, when the user reads it, then the user confirms every number is honest.
- A12 (R23). Given the first build, when the user and Claude walk through it, then every slide has been seen by the user as a rebuilt slide picture after its last change, and accepted by the user.
- A13 (R2, R3, R20, R24). Given the narrated rehearsal of the walked-through deck, when the user listens to it, then the user accepts it as a story told well. A listener outside data science, whom the user picks, can retell the story in three sentences after one listen.

### Adnan: is it true? and is it interesting?
- A14 (R21, R24). Given the draft and the numbers list sent to Adnan by 25 Oct 2026, when he reviews the numbers, then each of his comments on the facts is fixed or answered before submission.
- A15 (R21, R24). Given the same draft, when he reviews the story, then each of his comments on the telling is fixed or answered before submission.

### The conference team
- A16 (R21, R24). Given the reviewed PPTX and PDF sent to wateredgeil@mx.kinneret.ac.il by 1 Nov 2026, when the organisers receive them, then they are on time, in the requested format, fit the session, and are accepted into the program. The team cannot check our data, so their "is it true?" is that the deck is what was promised.

### Schedule
- A17 (R26). Given the calendar, when 20 Oct 2026 ends, then the first full build exists and the walk-through has started.

## Out of scope
- New modelling work. The freeze re-run and the persistence scoring are measurements of existing models and a standard baseline, not new models or horizons.
- A Hebrew version.
- Changes to the publication paper (the freeze numbers may later feed it, but not in this lane).
- Conference logistics, such as confirming the 10 Nov reception.
- Backup or appendix slides. The Q&A prep sheet covers Q&A. (Recommended answer to the intent's open question, for review.)

## Concerns
- The re-run and the persistence scoring sit right at the edge of "no new modelling work". I read them as measurement, not modelling. Reject this point if you see it differently.
- The champion retrains daily, so the working-tree `docs/olympics_results.json` (R² 0.762, retrained 2026-10-12, uncommitted) keeps moving. Since the date is flexible, the plan can either pin a dated run like that one or re-run all 8 models on one cut. Mixing runs from different dates is the one thing to avoid.
- The R² timeline mixes three kinds of change: model improvements (0.694 → 0.771, May–June), a stale-data reading (0.751), and the QC fix (0.764). Without labels it reads as instability, so its labels must be right.
- The Wade Storm gain (0.694 → 0.758) is historical. It was measured on 2026-05-31, and the 0.694 baseline was partly scored on corrupted 2023 rainfall data. It can't be re-measured on the freeze without rebuilding the pre-fix pipeline, so it conflicts with R9 unless it's labelled with its date. Resolved (originator, 2026-10-13): it is a dated historical step on the timeline, not reconstructed, and it is credited to the rain fix and the six fixes together.
- The GRU was retired from the training pipeline on 2026-06-01 (about 15 min per run on CPU). Its stored result is a frozen historical constant. Re-running it on the freeze (R10) means reviving the retired code path. The alternative is keeping its June result, labelled with its date. Both keep the punchline (R² ≈ 0). Resolved (originator, 2026-10-13): revive it and re-run it on the freeze, so all 8 models have real runtimes from one snapshot.
- R² is on daily volume change, not level. Stated loosely, technical listeners will ask about it (see A8, Q&A sheet).
- The 47 mm summer-recession result comes from a climatology method, not ML. If it's used in the long-term wall, it must be attributed that way.
- The 15-minute budget is tight for 8 segments. Trimming from the spine's 18 minutes is a design choice for the plan.

- The style rules (fonts, word counts, picture-only slides) are no longer tests that can fail the build. They can slip, so they rely on Claude's checklist and on the user's slide-by-slide walk-through to catch them.
- The 20 Oct first-build date is a proposal. It leaves five days of walk-through before 25 Oct, but the freeze re-run (with the GRU at about 15 min per run) and the images must land first.

## Open questions
- Intent: which data freeze date? Answered: 2026-10-11 by default, and flexible as needed (originator). The plan fixes the final date, and all numbers share it.
- Intent: Model Olympics models, R² and runtime? Answered in part: 8 configurations; R² from June/Oct runs exists (champion 0.762 today, GRU −0.026). Runtimes were never recorded and get measured on the freeze run (R10).
- Intent: gradient boosting vs persistence at 1 week? Never measured for the 7-day model (persistence appears only in the red-line back-test). Resolved by R11 on the freeze run.
- Intent: longer horizons and how they failed? Answered: 14–30 days, temperature only, has no year-round skill (dry season −1.23 vs climatology). The soil-moisture bucket lifts the wet season to about +0.35, a perfect-temperature upper bound. The summer red-line recession is skilful at 30 days (47 mm) but loses to plain climatology at 60–90 days. Source: paper §4.3–4.5.
- Intent: data sources and medallion layers? Answered: Water Authority level (1966–), Hydrological Service river flow (1969–), IMS meteorology with Open-Meteo fallback, and ERA5 reanalysis (1960–). Bronze = `Raw Data/`, Silver = `Silver Data/` (cleaned, daily-synchronised), Gold = `Gold Data/kinneret_gold_features.csv` (the fused model table, 2012–). Source: paper §2.
- Intent: missing or inaccessible data? Answered: no pumping, consumption or abstraction series at all. Also a 332-day outflow-station gap (Baptism Site 46105 → 316), a river-flow station gap (Obstacle Bridge, April 2026), a dead lake radiation sensor (25 Apr 2026), and dependence on third-party forecast APIs.
- Intent: 2–3 transferable challenges? Proposed for review: (1) bad data hides silently: a "-" sentinel corrupted inflow through a four-link chain, so you fix it at the source; (2) sensors and stations die: a retired station and a dead radiation sensor, so you build fallbacks; (3) the data you most need is often someone else's: pumping, so you share it. Resolved in the script, reviewed by the originator.
- Intent: current level vs red line, and the hook source? Answered: −213.655 m on 2026-10-11, 0.66 m below the −213.0 red line. The source is `Silver Data/Kinneret Level/kinneret_level.csv` (1966-09-01 → 2026-10-11).
- Intent: backup slides? Answered by recommendation: none, the Q&A sheet covers it (see Out of scope).
- Intent: which arc segments get trimmed to fit 15 minutes? Carried to the plan, which sets a per-segment time budget checked by A10.
- New: who is the outside listener for A13? Resolvable by the originator before rehearsal.
